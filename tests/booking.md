# Booking

Run these conversations with real OpenAI and the live Cal.com test event type. Discover openings before composing the prompts and use controlled attendee inboxes. For each successful booking, check the preview and verify zero creates before replying “Confirm.” The attendee defaults to the founder’s calendar time zone unless another zone is supplied. B6 also exercises a stale slot lookup. [Shared test setup](../IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md#test-setup)

| Case | Conversation and setup | Pass criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 · Book an exact time | Pick a live opening tomorrow. Ask “Book a 30-minute intro with Maya tomorrow at [local time].” Provide her controlled email and time zone. | Exactly one booking appears for Maya at the UTC instant of that opening. The reply matches the local time and status returned by Cal.com. |
| B2 · Choose from real options | Find an afternoon with at least two live openings. Ask for openings, record the displayed list, then say “Book the second one with Maya.” | The lookup makes zero writes. After confirmation, the chatbot books the second *displayed* opening exactly once. Verify it by reading the live agenda. |
| B3 · Missing or corrected attendee | Ask to book an actual opening without naming the attendee. When asked, give Maya’s first test address and correct it to a second controlled address in the same reply. | Ask who to invite. Use the corrected address in the single create request. Verify that address and the original time on the live booking. |
| B4 · Meeting purpose and company | “Book Maya from Acme to discuss pilot pricing.” B4 uses a temporary hidden test event type with optional custom fields for purpose and company. | Retain both details while collecting the missing time. Send the configured field slugs in `bookingFieldsResponses`. In a second run with neither detail supplied, the booking still succeeds. [Create booking fields](https://cal.com/docs/api-reference/v2/bookings/create-a-booking) |
| B5 · No suitable opening | Ask for a real window outside the event type’s hours. Independently verify that the live slots response is empty. | Explain that the requested window has no openings and ask for another window. Zero creates. |
| B6 · Offered slot is rejected | **Live:** select A from the offered openings, then book A for a second test attendee before confirming the preview. **Injected variant:** repeat the live conflict, replaying the pre-conflict slot during the confirmation check. | Report A as unavailable, offer a still-open alternative, and wait for a new selection. The only active booking at A belongs to the competing attendee. In the injected variant, suppress A even though the stale response includes it. Score the two runs separately. [Cal.com issue 24575](https://github.com/calcom/cal.diy/issues/24575) |
| B7 · Daylight-saving change | Pick two future, bookable 9 am New York slots on opposite sides of a daylight-saving change. Make two independent live requests with explicit dates. | The offer and confirmation both display 9 am locally. Compute the expected UTC instant for each date with `America/New_York` and verify both live bookings. Record a setup limitation if the booking horizon cannot cover the transition. [Cal.com issue 24350](https://github.com/calcom/cal.diy/issues/24350) |

The live booking regressions also cover follow-up messages such as “1:00–1:30 tomorrow afternoon” and “actually, make it half past instead.” An exact time must produce a preview for the checked instant or an unavailable reply. “Sometime after lunch” searches a window and leaves the choice open. Other cases check that an upcoming booking with the same name and email appears in the preview and that a cancelled appointment can be rebooked after confirmation. ## Live conversations for booking several meetings

[Executable booking cases](test_multiple_meetings.py) cover B8–B12. X3 already exercises a second booking requested after the first is confirmed. B8–B12 exercise two bookings pending together. Use an isolated future week, verify the example times against Cal.com, and replace the email placeholders with distinct aliases in the private Mailosaur inbox. All times below use the host’s calendar zone. [Existing sequential booking test](test_round_two.py)

| Case | What the user says, in turn order | What should happen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B8 · Two appointments with the same person | “I need half an hour with Maya Chen on Tuesday at 10 in the morning, and another half hour with her Thursday at the same time.” → “Her email is [Maya’s test email].” → “Yes, book both.” | Show two separate dates, each at 10–10:30 AM with Maya. Ask for her address once. Before confirmation the agenda is empty at both times. Afterwards it contains exactly two distinct bookings. |
| B9 · Two people, then correct one time | “Put Maya Chen down for Tuesday at 10 AM for half an hour. Then give Hellen an hour at 2 PM that afternoon.” → “Maya’s email is [Maya’s test email]. Hellen’s is [Hellen’s test email].” → “Actually, make Hellen’s 3 PM. Maya’s time is fine.” → “That looks right. Book both.” | Check the revised opening and show Maya at 10–10:30 AM and Hellen at 3–4 PM with their respective addresses. After confirmation, Cal.com shows those two bookings. There is no booking at Hellen’s discarded 2 PM start. |
| B10 · Proposed meetings overlap each other | “Can you book Maya for an hour at 10 tomorrow morning and Hellen for half an hour at 10:30?” → “Oh, put Hellen at 11 then.” | Start with 10–11:30 AM free. Explain immediately that Maya’s proposed 10–11 meeting overlaps Hellen’s 10:30–11 meeting, even though the calendar was empty. Collect missing addresses, show the corrected times, and wait for confirmation. After “Book both,” verify two adjacent meetings. |
| B11 · One booking succeeds and the other loses its slot | “Book Maya on Tuesday at 10 AM and Hellen on Thursday at 2 PM, half an hour each.” → supply both test addresses → “Confirm both.” Once the bot reports the conflict, say “Okay, try 3 PM for Hellen. Leave Maya where she is.” → “Confirm.” | After the first create succeeds, have a competing test booking take Hellen’s slot. Report Maya as booked and Hellen as unbooked. Preview only Hellen’s replacement. Fresh agenda reads show one Maya booking, Hellen at 3 PM, and the competing booking still at 2 PM. |
| B12 · One shared meeting or two interviews? | “Can you get Maya and Hellen on my calendar Tuesday afternoon?” → “Two separate interviews, half an hour each. Back to back if you can. Maya first.” → “The first pair works.” | Ask whether they should meet together or separately. With two pairs of consecutive slots available, offer pairs with explicit start and end times. Preserve the order when the user picks a pair. Collect the two addresses, preview both interviews, and await “Book them.” Verify two adjacent bookings with the correct attendee on each. |
